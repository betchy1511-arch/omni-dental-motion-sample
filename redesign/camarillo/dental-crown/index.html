Crowns explained for Camarillo

Why a Tooth Might Need a Crown

Think of a crown as a cap, made in the shape of a tooth, that goes over one with decay, a break, weakness or heavy wear. Because it wraps around the whole tooth, the dentist first removes a little enamel so it fits.

Once it is cemented on, the crown takes over for your natural tooth above the gum line (opens in a new tab). That can give a weak tooth more support, return it to its usual shape and size, or simply make it look better.

Crowns have a few other uses as well. One can hold a dental bridge in place or hide a tooth with heavy staining. A crown also protects a tooth treated with a root canal, and it is the part you see on a dental implant.

Step by step

How the Dental Crown Process Works

The dental crown procedure requires at least two visits: first for preparing the tooth and taking impressions, then a second appointment for placing the permanent crown after laboratory fabrication.

  1. 01

    Comprehensive Exam

    Your journey begins with a comprehensive examination where Dr. Keihani evaluates your oral health and determines if a dental crown is the appropriate solution.

  2. 02

    Preparing the Tooth

    During your first treatment appointment, he carefully prepares the affected tooth by removing decay and reshaping it to accommodate the crown, with local anesthesia ensuring your comfort throughout.

  3. 03

    Digital Impressions

    Omni Dental utilizes advanced digital technology to create highly accurate impressions of your prepared tooth. Your digital impressions are sent to our trusted dental laboratory, where skilled technicians craft your permanent crown using the material selected during your consultation. While your permanent crown is being fabricated, a temporary crown protects your prepared tooth.

  4. 04

    Placing Your Crown

    When your permanent crown is ready, you’ll return for the final placement. Dr. Keihani removes the temporary crown, thoroughly cleans the prepared tooth, and carefully checks the fit and appearance of your new crown before permanently bonding it to your tooth using dental cement.

Throughout the entire dental crown procedure, our team prioritizes your comfort and satisfaction. We welcome questions at any stage and strive to make your experience as pleasant and stress-free as possible.

Care and maintenance

Caring for Your Dental Crown

Maintaining excellent oral hygiene is crucial for dental crown longevity.

Brush twice daily with a soft-bristled toothbrush and fluoride toothpaste, paying special attention to the crown-tooth margin. Floss daily around your crown using a gentle sawing motion. For enhanced cleaning, consider using an interdental brush or water flosser to remove debris from hard-to-reach areas around your dental crown. Exercise caution with sticky foods like caramel or taffy that might adhere to and pull on crowns. Limit consumption of hard foods like nuts or hard candy, especially if you have porcelain crowns. Avoid biting hard objects like ice, pens, or fingernails, as these can damage both natural teeth and dental restorations.

If you grind or clench your teeth, Dr. Keihani may recommend a custom nightguard to protect your dental crown from excessive pressure. Schedule biannual examinations at Omni Dental Specialty to allow Dr. Keihani to evaluate your crown’s condition and address any concerns before they become significant problems. Contact our office immediately if you notice any changes in your crown, such as looseness, discomfort when biting, or visible damage.

Crown types

Dental Crown Materials for Camarillo Patients

  • Same-Day CEREC Crowns

    Using advanced CAD/CAM technology, Omni Dental can create and place ceramic crowns in a single visit, eliminating the need for temporary crowns and multiple appointments.

  • Porcelain or Ceramic

    Ideal for front teeth where aesthetics are paramount, porcelain crowns are excellent for patients with metal allergies and provide outstanding dental restoration results.

  • Porcelain-Fused-to-Metal

    Combining durability with aesthetics, PFM crowns feature a metal base for strength with a porcelain exterior for natural appearance.

  • Metal

    Composed of gold, platinum, or base-metal alloys, metal crowns offer unparalleled durability.

  • Zirconia

    Highly resistant to fracture and wear, zirconia crowns are suitable for patients who grind their teeth.

03Should my tooth get a filling or a crown?

The amount of healthy tooth structure remaining is a primary consideration when deciding between these treatments. Dental fillings work best for smaller areas of decay where the majority of the tooth remains intact. Dental crowns become necessary when decay or damage affects a significant portion of the tooth, typically more than 50%.

04Which crown material should I pick?

Different crown materials offer varying balances of cost, aesthetics, and durability. Dr. Keihani will help you select the optimal crown type based on your specific needs, preferences, and budget constraints.
