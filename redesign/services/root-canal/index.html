How a root canal works

Understanding Root Canal Treatment

A root canal saves a damaged tooth by cleaning out the pulp and nerves inside it that have been affected by bacteria, deep decay or injury. The canals are then cleaned, disinfected and filled with gutta-percha so the tooth keeps working.

Common causes include deep decay, repeated dental procedures on the same tooth, or trauma. You may need treatment if you have persistent discomfort, prolonged sensitivity to heat or cold, tenderness when chewing, swollen gums, or a pimple-like bump near a tooth.

Ignoring these signs can lead to a spreading infection, severe pain and eventually losing the tooth. If you notice them, an evaluation is the best next step for your oral and overall health.

Step by step

How the Root Canal Process Works

A root canal is a precise procedure that removes damaged pulp from inside your tooth. Here is a simple overview of the steps.

  1. 01

    Numbing the Area

    A local anesthetic completely numbs the tooth and the area around it, so you stay comfortable.

  2. 02

    Cleaning the Canal

    A small opening is made in the tooth to reach and remove the damaged or infected pulp from the root canal system.

  3. 03

    Filling and Sealing

    The canals are thoroughly cleaned, disinfected and filled with a biocompatible material such as gutta-percha. The opening is sealed with a temporary filling.

  4. 04

    Final Restoration

    The tooth then gets a permanent restoration, typically a crown, which protects it and restores its full function.

Most root canal treatments are completed in one or two visits, depending on how complex your case is, and you get detailed post-treatment care instructions.

Why patients choose a root canal

Why a Root Canal Is Worth It

A root canal relieves the pain caused by an infected or inflamed tooth and lets you keep your natural tooth. With modern techniques, the process is comfortable and highly effective.

  • Keeps your natural tooth. Saving the tooth helps prevent tooth loss.
  • Relieves pain. Treatment removes the source of the pain inside the tooth.
  • Stops infection spreading. Infected tissue is removed and the canals are sealed.
  • Normal chewing. With a crown on top, the tooth can work like it did before.

Comfort and recovery

Comfort and Recovery After a Root Canal

Thanks to modern anesthetics and gentle techniques, a root canal today is typically no more uncomfortable than a routine filling.

After your procedure you may have mild sensitivity, which typically settles within a few days. It is important to follow up with a permanent restoration, usually a crown, to protect the tooth and restore its full function. You get detailed post-treatment care instructions to support a smooth recovery.

Strict sterilization protocols protect your health during every procedure. If you feel anxious about dental work, gentle anesthesia and additional sedation options are available, and every step is explained in clear language.

01Is root canal therapy painful?

Thanks to modern anesthetics and gentle techniques, the treatment is typically no more uncomfortable than a routine filling. The procedure actually relieves the pain caused by the infected or inflamed tooth, and sedation options are available if you have dental anxiety.

02Will I need more than one visit?

Most root canal treatments are completed in one or two visits, depending on how complex your case is.

03How do I know if I need an emergency root canal appointment?

Intense, persistent tooth pain, swelling of the gums or face, sensitivity to hot or cold, or a pimple-like bump on your gums can point to an infection that should not wait. Call the office right away for a prompt evaluation.

04What should I expect after treatment?

You may have mild sensitivity that typically settles within a few days. The tooth then needs a permanent restoration, usually a crown, to protect it and restore its full function. You get detailed care instructions to support your recovery.

05What if a previous root canal did not work?

Root canal retreatment may help when an earlier root canal did not fully clear infection or pain. The existing filling material is removed, the inside of the tooth is cleaned again, and any missed canals are treated and sealed, under local anesthesia.

06How much does a root canal cost, and does insurance cover it?

The cost depends on the tooth involved and how complex the case is. You get a clear breakdown of fees at your consultation, the team will gladly verify your insurance coverage, and flexible payment options and financing plans are available.

07Do I need a referral to book an appointment?

You do not always need a referral. If you have symptoms of a tooth that may need a root canal, or you have been advised to get one, you can call the office directly to book.
