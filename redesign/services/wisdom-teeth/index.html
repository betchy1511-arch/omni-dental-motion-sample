What wisdom teeth are

Understanding Wisdom Teeth

Wisdom teeth are your third set of molars, usually coming in during late adolescence or early adulthood. They get their name because they develop at a more mature age than your other teeth.

Some people never have a problem with them. Many others do, because the jaw does not have enough space or the teeth come in incorrectly.

A wisdom tooth can stay trapped beneath the gums or only partly come through. That can cause discomfort and affect the teeth and tissues around it. Whether you are in discomfort or want your wisdom teeth evaluated, Dr. Keihani and the team are here to help.

Why removal helps

Why Remove Problem Wisdom Teeth

Removing problematic wisdom teeth can help prevent pain, infection and long-term complications.

  • Less pain and infection. Problem wisdom teeth can cause discomfort and raise infection risk.
  • Protect nearby teeth. Crowding or pushing against neighboring teeth can cause damage.
  • Fewer cavities and gum problems. Hard-to-clean wisdom teeth are prone to decay and inflammation.
  • Room for orthodontics. Removal can keep them from interfering with braces or other treatments.

Your extraction

Simple and Surgical Extractions

The way a wisdom tooth is removed depends on how it has come in.

For a fully erupted wisdom tooth, a simple extraction technique is used to loosen and remove the tooth. For an impacted wisdom tooth that is trapped beneath the gums or only partly through, a small incision is made in the gum to reach and remove it.

Every procedure begins with a local anesthetic to numb the area. Nitrous oxide is available for added relaxation, and IV sedation can be provided on request, depending on your needs.

Aftercare

Aftercare Following Your Extraction

  • Rest and a Ride Home

    Arrange for a friend or family member to drive you home, since you will still be recovering from anesthesia.

  • Gauze

    Keep the gauze in place for at least 30 minutes, or up to a day if needed.

  • Swelling

    Ice packs help keep swelling down. Steroids may be prescribed if necessary.

  • Pain Relief

    Over-the-counter pain medication is usually enough. Stronger medication may be prescribed after a surgical extraction.

  • Gentle Cleaning

    Rinse with warm salt water 24 hours after the procedure, and brush and floss carefully around the site.

01Do wisdom teeth always need to come out?

No. Some people have no issues with their wisdom teeth. Removal is often needed when the jaw lacks space, the teeth are impacted, or they cause pain, infection, decay, gum disease or damage to nearby teeth.

02What does it mean when a wisdom tooth is impacted?

An impacted wisdom tooth stays trapped beneath the gums or only partly erupts. It is removed with a surgical extraction, where a small incision is made in the gum to reach the tooth.

03Will I be numb or sedated?

The procedure begins with a local anesthetic to numb the area. Nitrous oxide is available for added relaxation, and IV sedation can be provided on request, depending on your needs.

04Can I drive myself home afterward?

Plan for a friend or family member to drive you home, because you will still be recovering from anesthesia.

05How long should I keep the gauze in?

Keep the gauze in place for at least 30 minutes, or up to a day if needed.

06When can I rinse and brush again?

Rinse with warm salt water 24 hours after the procedure to keep the sockets clean and moist. Brush and floss carefully, avoiding the extraction site.
