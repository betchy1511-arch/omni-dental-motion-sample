How a crown works

Understanding Dental Crowns

A dental crown is a tooth-shaped cap that completely covers a damaged tooth to restore its shape, size, strength and appearance. Dr. Keihani evaluates each patient carefully to recommend the most appropriate type of crown.

The amount of healthy tooth left is a key part of that decision. Fillings work best for smaller areas of decay where most of the tooth is intact. A crown becomes necessary when decay or damage affects a significant portion of the tooth, typically more than 50%.

Molars and premolars, which take heavy chewing forces, often benefit from the complete protection of a crown when they are moderately to severely damaged. Your entire crown procedure happens under one roof, with no referrals needed.

Step by step

How the Dental Crown Process Works

A traditional crown takes at least two visits: one to prepare the tooth and take impressions, and one to place the permanent crown.

  1. 01

    Comprehensive Exam

    Dr. Keihani evaluates your oral health and confirms whether a crown is the right solution for the tooth.

  2. 02

    Preparing the Tooth

    Decay is removed and the tooth is reshaped to hold the crown, with local anesthesia for your comfort.

  3. 03

    Digital Impressions

    Highly accurate digital impressions go to a trusted dental laboratory, and a temporary crown protects your tooth while the permanent one is made.

  4. 04

    Placing Your Crown

    The temporary crown comes off, the tooth is cleaned, and the fit and look of your new crown are checked before it is bonded in place with dental cement.

Prefer a single visit? With CAD/CAM technology, same-day CEREC ceramic crowns can be created and placed in one appointment, with no temporary crown.

Care and maintenance

Caring for Your Dental Crown

Good daily oral hygiene is the key to a long-lasting crown.

Brush twice a day with a soft-bristled toothbrush and fluoride toothpaste, paying special attention to where the crown meets the tooth, and floss daily around it. An interdental brush or water flosser helps clean hard-to-reach areas. Be careful with sticky foods like caramel or taffy, limit hard foods like nuts or hard candy, and avoid biting ice, pens or fingernails.

If you grind or clench your teeth, Dr. Keihani may recommend a custom nightguard. Visit twice a year so your crown can be checked, and call the office right away if it feels loose, hurts when you bite, or looks damaged.

Crown types

Dental Crown Materials in Oxnard

  • Same-Day CEREC Crowns

    Ceramic crowns designed with CAD/CAM technology and placed in a single visit, with no temporary crown.

  • Porcelain or Ceramic

    The most natural look, ideal for front teeth and for patients with metal allergies.

  • Porcelain-Fused-to-Metal

    A metal base for strength with a porcelain exterior, for front or back teeth.

  • Metal

    Gold, platinum or base-metal alloys that resist wear, with minimal tooth removal.

  • Zirconia

    Strong and natural looking, highly resistant to fracture, and suitable if you grind your teeth.

01How long do dental crowns last?

High-quality crowns typically last 10 to 15 years or longer with proper care, including daily brushing and flossing and checkups twice a year.

02Can I get a crown in one visit?

Yes. Using CAD/CAM technology, same-day CEREC ceramic crowns can be created and placed in a single visit, with no temporary crown and no second appointment. A traditional lab-made crown takes at least two visits.

03Do I need a crown or a filling?

It depends on how much healthy tooth is left. Fillings work best for smaller areas of decay. A crown becomes necessary when decay or damage affects a significant portion of the tooth, typically more than 50%.

04Which crown material is right for me?

Each material balances cost, appearance and durability differently. Porcelain suits front teeth, metal and zirconia are very strong, and porcelain-fused-to-metal works for front or back teeth. Dr. Keihani will help you choose based on your needs, preferences and budget.

05What affects the cost, and do you take insurance?

The cost depends on the material, how complex your case is, and any treatment needed before the crown is placed. The office works with most major dental insurance providers and offers flexible financing options for patients without insurance or with limited coverage.

06What are your office hours?

The office is open Monday to Thursday from 9am to 6pm and Friday from 8am to 5pm. It is closed on Saturday and Sunday.
