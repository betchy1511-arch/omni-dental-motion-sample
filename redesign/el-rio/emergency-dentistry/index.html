Know When to Call 911 or Go to an Emergency Room

Some general safety points can help you choose where to go first, and a dentist can tell you what fits your own situation. For dental care, the office is about 2 miles from El Rio, and Google Maps puts the drive at about 8 to 9 minutes by car via N. Rose Avenue, depending on traffic.

  • Pain with fever and chills. Unbearable tooth pain along with fever and chills is a dental emergency. Call a dentist or go to the nearest emergency room right away. Though it is rare, an infection in your mouth can spread to other areas of your body.
  • Swelling, a knot or a fever. swelling below your eye, a knot on your jaw and a fever over 101 degrees Fahrenheit (opens in a new tab) are reasons to go to an emergency room. Swelling in the face or jaw is a reason to contact a dentist.
  • Bleeding or pain that will not ease. bleeding that will not stop with applied pressure, and unbearable tooth pain that does not improve with medication, are also reasons to go to an emergency room.
  • A possible broken jaw. If you cannot close your upper and lower teeth together, your jaw may be broken. This needs medical help right away: call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room.
  • After a fall or injury. After a tooth is knocked out, contact a dentist or go to an emergency room as soon as possible. After a hard fall, a provider may also check for a concussion.

Step by step

What to Do if a Tooth Is Knocked Out

When a tooth gets knocked out, time is of the essence. Omni Dental Specialty provides expert care for knocked-out teeth, offering your best chance at saving your natural tooth.

  1. 01

    Pick It Up by the Crown

    Retrieve the tooth, handling it only by the crown (not the root).

  2. 02

    Rinse Gently

    Gently rinse it with water if dirty (don’t scrub or remove attached tissue).

  3. 03

    Put It Back or Keep It Moist

    Try to reinsert it in the socket if possible. If reinsertion isn’t possible, keep the tooth moist in milk or saliva.

  4. 04

    Call Right Away

    Call our Oxnard dental office immediately at (805) 366-6000.

With prompt emergency dental treatment, our experienced dentist may be able to reimplant and stabilize your natural tooth.

Before your visit

What to Do Until You Are Seen

When a dental emergency strikes, knowing what to do can make the difference between saving and losing a tooth.

Severe toothache: Rinse with warm salt water. Gently floss to remove any trapped food. Apply a cold compress for swelling. Take over-the-counter pain medication as directed. Cracked or broken tooth: Rinse your mouth with warm water. Apply a cold compress to reduce swelling. Cover any sharp edges with dental wax if available. Lost filling or crown: Keep the area clean by gently rinsing with warm salt water. If you have the crown, you can temporarily reattach it using dental cement from a pharmacy. Avoid chewing on that side of your mouth.

For serious, uncontrolled bleeding, go directly to the emergency room, then follow up with our Oxnard dentist for comprehensive care. A dental abscess is not just a dental emergency. It can become a medical emergency if the infection spreads.

03What signs can point to an emergency root canal?

Severe, persistent toothache. Extreme sensitivity to hot and cold. Swelling around the affected tooth. Tenderness when touching the tooth or chewing. Darkening of the tooth. Pimple-like bump on the gums near the painful tooth.

04What does treatment for a dental abscess involve?

A dental abscess is a pocket of pus caused by bacterial infection, often resulting in severe pain, swelling, and potential health complications if left untreated. Immediate pain management. Drainage of the abscess. Antibiotic therapy when necessary. Root canal treatment to save the affected tooth. Extraction for severely damaged teeth. Follow-up care to prevent recurrence.

05What are the repair options for a broken or chipped tooth?

Dental bonding for minor chips. Porcelain veneers for front tooth damage. Dental crowns for severely broken teeth. Root canal therapy when damage extends to the dental pulp. Extraction and replacement options for irreparable damage.

07What helps prevent a dental emergency?

Maintain regular dental check-ups and cleanings every six months. Wear a custom mouthguard during sports and physical activities. Use a nightguard if you grind or clench your teeth. Avoid using your teeth as tools to open packages or bottles. Don’t chew on ice, popcorn kernels, or hard candies. Address small dental issues before they become emergency situations.
