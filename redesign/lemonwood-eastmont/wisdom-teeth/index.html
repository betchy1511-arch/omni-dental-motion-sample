The last molars

Why Wisdom Teeth Arrive Late

These are the third molars, the final teeth to come in. Most people see them in their late teens or early adult years, and they got their name because they form later in life than your other teeth.

Plenty of people keep theirs and never have a problem. For others the jaw lacks space, or the teeth erupt at a bad angle. Either one can hurt and can cause trouble for nearby teeth and tissue.

Step by step

How Wisdom Teeth Removal Works

Dr. Keihani prioritizes patient comfort and safety during the extraction process.

  1. 01

    Exam and X-ray

    The dentist examines your mouth and takes an X-ray before deciding on treatment. Tell your dentist about any pain or changes you have noticed.

  2. 02

    Talking Through Options

    You and your dentist discuss the course of treatment together. Options range from removal to watching the teeth over time, which is called active surveillance.

  3. 03

    Deciding Whether to Remove

    Common reasons for removing a tooth include impacted teeth that are causing problems, such as wisdom teeth. Deep infection, overcrowding and gum disease are other reasons.

  4. 04

    Numbing the Area

    Tooth removal starts with a local anesthetic that numbs the area. The tooth is then loosened and taken out. Contact the dental office with questions about this step.

  5. 05

    Healing at Home

    Allow 1 to 2 weeks for the socket to heal, and longer for the bone and tissue around it. Skip straws for at least 24 hours. Sucking hard through a straw can loosen the blood clot and lead to a dry socket (opens in a new tab).

Caring for wisdom teeth

Practical Notes on Caring for Your Wisdom Teeth

Before you talk with a dentist about wisdom teeth, a few general points can help. Which ones apply is a question for your visit. See the full list of dental services for Lemonwood and Eastmont patients.

  • If wisdom teeth stay in place. Wisdom teeth that are not removed should still be monitored, because problems can develop later. Floss around them and visit the dentist regularly.
  • Pain is not the only sign. A tooth can have disease even when it does not hurt. This is one reason regular checkups matter even when nothing hurts.
  • When to see your dentist. A little discomfort is normal when wisdom teeth appear, but if you have pain, see your dentist immediately. Tell your dentist what you notice and when it started.
  • Things to avoid after removal. One risk is the blood clot falling out of the socket in the days after the procedure. Do not smoke or use tobacco after removal.
  • Cold compresses and soft foods. A cold compress on the cheek can ease swelling and pain. Ask your dentist which foods to avoid while you heal.

Our approach

Your extraction

Simple and Surgical Extractions

Dr. Keihani prioritizes patient comfort and safety during the extraction process.

For fully erupted wisdom teeth, a simple extraction technique is used to loosen and remove the tooth. For impacted wisdom teeth, a small incision is made in the gum to access and remove the tooth.

The procedure begins with a local anesthetic to numb the area. Nitrous oxide is available for added relaxation, and IV sedation can be provided upon request, depending on your needs.

Aftercare

Aftercare Following Your Extraction

  • Rest and a Ride Home

    Arrange for a friend or family member to drive you home, as you’ll still be recovering from anesthesia.

  • Gauze

    Keep gauze in place for at least 30 minutes, or up to a day if needed.

  • Swelling

    Use ice packs to minimize swelling, and your provider may prescribe steroids if necessary.

  • Pain Relief

    Over-the-counter pain medication is typically sufficient, though stronger painkillers may be prescribed for surgical extractions.

  • Gentle Cleaning

    Rinse with warm salt water 24 hours after the procedure to keep the sockets clean and moist. Brush and floss carefully, avoiding the extraction site.

01Does every wisdom tooth need removing?

While some people experience no issues with their wisdom teeth, many others face complications due to lack of space or improper eruption, which can affect surrounding teeth and tissues. Removing problematic wisdom teeth can help prevent pain, infection, and long-term complications.

02What makes a wisdom tooth impacted?

Wisdom teeth remain trapped beneath the gums or partially erupt. For impacted wisdom teeth, a small incision is made in the gum to access and remove the tooth.

03Which comfort options are there for the procedure?

The procedure begins with a local anesthetic to numb the area. Nitrous oxide is available for added relaxation, and IV sedation can be provided upon request, depending on your needs.
